# Cognitive Development in Adolescence

## Summary Overview
During adolescence, teenagers typically transition from concrete operational thinking to formal operational thought. This means they develop the ability to think abstractly, reason logically about hypothetical situations, and employ metacognition (thinking about their own thinking).

## Key Concepts
- **Abstract Thinking:** The ability to understand concepts that cannot be seen or touched (e.g., justice, hypothetical futures).
- **Metacognition:** Reflecting on one's own thought processes, leading to increased self-awareness but sometimes egocentrism.
- **Risk-Taking:** Due to the asynchronous development of the prefrontal cortex (responsible for executive function and impulse control) and the limbic system (reward processing), adolescents are biologically predisposed to seek novel, high-reward experiences, which sometimes translates into increased risk-taking behavior.
