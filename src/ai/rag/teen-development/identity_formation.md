# Identity Formation in Adolescence

Adolescence is a critical period for identity formation. As teens develop cognitively, they begin to think more abstractly about themselves and their place in the world.

## Key Principles:
1. **Exploration is normal:** Teens often try on different personas, interests, and friend groups. This is a healthy part of finding out who they are.
2. **Peer influence:** The shift from family-centric to peer-centric social lives is expected. Peer validation becomes a strong motivator.
3. **Individuation:** Teens need space to express opinions different from their parents to build a sense of autonomy.

## Navigating Challenges:
When teens face high expectations (e.g., academic pressure) alongside identity confusion, it can manifest as anxiety or withdrawal. Validation of their unique strengths (e.g., highlighting their creative or social abilities rather than just academic performance) builds resilience.
